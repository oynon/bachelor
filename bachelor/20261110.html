2026-10-10: I MADE A GAME!

INTRO

Throughout October 2026—and the allotted pre-planning period over the month of September—I kind of disappeared from social media and pretty much every other aspect of my life in order to work on what I would consider to be a major project: my first video game, Have Mercy On Me. :] This was a solo project made for the Denpa VN Jam over on itch.io, which was also my first game jam(!). I had a lot of ‘firsts’ this month :P

This post may not be super valuable to you if you aren’t already interested in either development, the creative process or the game itself, but I think either way that it’s pretty useful and fun to document something that’s such a big milestone for me. I hope that any other people who stumble across this can potentially find some inspiration here if nothing else :)

IN THE BEGINNING

In around June 2025 (yes you read this right), I came up with a very, very loose plot skeleton for what would (eventually, much further in the future) become Have Mercy On Me. I was on a walk outside and decided I was sick of the characters/stories I had and wanted a weird, radical change. Off the top of my head, I came up with this quick concept about a shut-in being “saved” by a mysterious, supernatural being. I wasn’t doing well mentally at the time and wanted to take on a new project to help myself feel better about it.

At that time, I was watching a lot of Welcome to the NHK and I think I’d just finished Chaos;Head (lol), so there were a lot of things in that vein at the forefront of my mind. I knew I wanted this to eventually become a visual novel, but I had kind of relegated it to the depths of my mind because 1) I’d assumed this would just never be able to come to fruition. I’m not a confident artist and I felt like I’d be letting people down by not delivering powerful visuals 2) the “plot”, or what little there was of it, just felt like it was missing something.

The idea of a social outcast being confronted by an otherworldly figure was cool, but it wasn’t a plot on its own, right? I did a little bit more thinking about it at this point (still not really giving much thought to the characters’ personalities and only focusing on the logistics, how I could possibly make two characters like this meet, etc) and came up with a third character for the story who is no longer relevant or extant but who I vaguely think was named Hakuga or something like that. In this initial “beta” concept, Taro was a high school dropout with no other prospects and no juicy past, just a lot of mental health issues and anxiety about the world. Kyrie wasn’t an alien. I don’t really remember what they were.

Instead of being able to change body parts into metal objects, Kyrie had an intense healing power similar to kintsugi; the idea here was that after meeting Kyrie and seeing them heal a minor injury, Taro would become obsessed with having Kyrie “fix” his flaws, physical and spiritual. This other character, Hakuga, was a version of Taro from the future who had traveled back in time to warn him about this. He was also romanceable which is one of the funniest things about this period in time. Taro and Kyrie’s names were the same as they are now, except for the fact that Taro Yamada at this point wasn’t an alias and was just his name. Something else that I remember / just find interesting is that Kyrie’s design hasn’t really changed at all since that time. I always envisioned them with green hair and warm eyes. I obsessed over this excuse for a “story” for like a single week, then forgot about it and only really thought of it incidentally.

About a year later, in June or July of 2026, I started thinking about these characters again. I had dealt with some very upsetting major life changes and, again, returned to creating and thinking about characters. Writing, for me, is definitely an “escape” in the sense that it gives me something to do when I otherwise feel like giving up on everything. Just to give you an idea of where the story came from, when I started thinking about HMOM again back in June, I was very physically ill and actively suicidal. At that point, I decided I wanted to try and riff on the existing characters I had made. I shrunk the cast (removing Hakuga), decided I wanted a story with only two characters, and intended on writing about suicide.

Again, I started thinking about these characters and again, I didn’t really get much done. I reworked Taro’s character into someone who had been successful/happy in the past and had since become someone he wasn’t willing to deal with (an experience I felt like I personally resonated with and could write candidly about) and removed the “healer” aspect from Kyrie’s character in order to rework them into someone who “destroyed” instead of created. If the early version of HMOM was focused on growth, “adding” things to make something perfect and an excess of life, then this intermediate version was retooled to be about death, decay and excision. I became obsessed with the idea of what kind of person would want to remove things from themselves instead of add them and came to the conclusion that Taro and Kyrie would need to be two parts of a whole. Taro—who had previously been a passive character with a meek personality—turned into the type of person I envisioned when I thought of a person who had “lost everything”. He was radically apathetic, suicidal, self-loathing, self-annihilating, impatient, a perfectionist and desperate for change.

I realized that, honestly, what I had really wanted all along was a change and that what had really bothered me about the first iteration of Taro was that his character was a blank canvas. He was a person with no life experiences, no grand tragedies and no real desires. He hated himself, but it was because of how boring and miserable his life was. After doing some thinking, I realized that many suicidal people do (or did) have pretty rich, engaging lives at some point and either lost everything that gave them meaning or threw it all away. I couldn’t connect to original!taro as a character because of how empty his world was. I had to give him some emotional stakes.

In July, I’d intended on making the game real, but eventually just got too distracted by other things and also paralyzed by my own thoughts/feelings/indecision. It wasn’t the right time for me to come up with a story to tell about these characters, even if my interest in them had been piqued again by the minor reworking I’d done to the concept. More than anything it was a relatively-plotless series of extremely complex AMVs I thought about in my head. I had Themes And Such but no real story other than a Misery-esque “hikikomori former celebrity is held hostage by an alien, sort of”. I moved on.

In late August, I ended up hearing about the Denpa VN Jam. I wasn’t really sure at first. I’d never been part of a jam before and was worried about being judged. I also didn’t know anyone and didn’t have any samples of my work, so I was concerned I wouldn’t be able to find a team. I ended up joining anyway, just for the sake of it.

While I was working on a different project with a cool group I’d found, my mind just kind of ended up wandering back to HMOM no matter what I did. It was really weird. In that way, I think I almost got this “calling” telling me to work on it, like if I didn’t do it for the jam then I probably never would. I figured I’d try to do some small writing exercises with the characters just to find my bearings and see if I wanted to commit to it.

I did. :)

THE WRITING PROCESS

Have Mercy On Me isn’t an autobiographical story, but it’s definitely not one that I think I ever could have written at all if I hadn’t been as lonely and sick as I was this year. My desire to see it realized was exacerbated by my memories of the things I’d either done to myself or done to other people; in simpler words, writing this was really painful, to say the least. More often than not, it was a challenge to actualize the story I wanted to tell because the process of telling it at all meant thinking and speaking as the perspective character (who is very troubled), which meant delving into parts of my mind that hurt me to delve into. I think I wrote it at an interesting time in my life where I had a lot of very fresh traumatic memories I hadn’t processed and kind of worked them out internally as I tried to figure out what I wanted to do with the narrative. As I said, it was hard to put myself into the shoes of someone who is so suicidal, not because I didn’t understand where he was coming from but because I think I’ve been there too many times to count. Writing for Taro felt like opening a wound in myself… even if our wounds aren’t exactly the same, they resemble each other.

I procrastinated a lot and didn’t make good use of my time. I’m obsessive-compulsive (I imagine this is probably way more than you ever wanted to know about me) and have this ritual where I go on long, long walks, usually in the late afternoon or even close to midnight, and that was when I’d do a lot of my thinking about the game. I did less actual development/writing than walking during the first part of the month. I want to say I didn’t actually start “locking in” on writing until around October 15th and I was certain for most of the jam period that I wouldn’t finish at all. The scope of the game changed four times (twenty-one days to seven days to five days, then back to seven once I realized I had too much story to tell).

There were a lot of times when I doubted myself and wanted to give up entirely. A thought I had more than ten times was, “I can’t publish this”. Something about it felt devilish, like just humiliatingly “taboo” to the point where I feared I’d be laughed off stage, basically. I think it was probably something to do with how brazen the MC can be about the topics of suicide and mental health. Mental health isn’t an under-discussed topic in denpa and there are definitely VNs out there that talk about suicide, but I think the level of straightforwardness about the actual act and idea of “killing yourself” in HMOM isn’t really something I see that often, especially in English-language games. Suicide, self-harm and mental health are still stigmatized topics globally, so it was kind of scary (in a good way) to write something with such a blatant focus on a character who was really struggling and so many explicit references to the act. When I was at my lowest this year, I really felt like there was nowhere that I could turn to and no one who would listen to me because I’d burnt all my bridges. I felt like there was no exit to the hell I’d made for myself. While I was working on HMOM, I wondered if there were other people out there who’ve ever felt this way. This was part of what motivated me to finish it. I wanted to see if anyone out there felt similarly to Taro, as if they’d just been “created differently”, like a defective model, and needed to die as a result.

It was important to me to portray his mental health issues realistically and honestly, even when the situation seemed embarrassing, ugly or sad. Mental health can be a hard topic to write about because the last thing I want is to hurt someone in real life without meaning to, and mental health & suicide can be sensitive topics for a lot of people. I tried to portray Taro as someone with a lot of loyalty, a big heart and a strong desire to fit in, because I think people with low empathy or ‘disordered’ senses of self-worth are often misconstrued as being heartless people. I definitely don’t think that’s the case, and it’s important to me to write stories where even people who are often portrayed as monsters by popular culture can be seen in an objective, fair and empathetic way. I interpret him as being someone who’s easily led to temptation and has to consistently choose to be kind and patient over being mean and quick to anger, which is usually the “easier” path but can end up causing a lot of harm to a lot of people. He’s driven, in my opinion, by fear: fear of being forgotten, fear of being abandoned, fear of stagnation, fear of ridicule, etc.

Even his fear of water ties back to this idea I’d had of someone who (both physically and psychologically) lives on an island. The bulk of Have Mercy On Me takes place in Osaka, Japan, which I didn’t realize until relatively late in development is actually called the “City of Water” because of how it’s crisscrossed by rivers and canals, like Venice or Alto Mare from the Pokemon Latios and Latias movie(this is a really weird reference sorry. I probably watched this 100 times as a kid). Taro lived in New York City—famously an island—for ten years and was born way, way out in what I would consider to be a pretty isolated part of Kochi Prefecture. Partially, this is just because I like water. I think it’s a great thing to write about and creates a strong visual image. At the same time, though, it’s a little enticing to imagine this guy who’s spent his whole life feeling isolated from other people—who feels as if he was “born defective” and can’t be rehabilitated into normalcy—spending the entirety of his real, physical life surrounded by barriers that he can’t cross. It might be simple for someone to get on a ferry and travel from one place to another, but that’s something Taro finds great difficulty in due to his aquaphobia and the juxtaposition between that psychological sensation of feeling “stranded” and the physical sensation of being stuck on an island is one I really dig. In a lot of ways, I think HMOM can be compared to Metal Gear Solid 2 (2001) (one of my favorite video games of all time) and this is for sure one of them. The game design there is just terrific, and I’ve always really appreciated how from the very first second of the game—that shot of Snake on the Brooklyn Bridge in the rain—you’re surrounded by water, then they cut to the scenes on the tanker in the Hudson, then they cut to the plant chapter in the middle of the ocean, then there’s a scene in a room with an indoor pool featuring a character who can’t swim, it’s just all-around a lot of awesomeness where Kojima plays around with this idea of water as a psychological (or even dissociative) barrier between characters. Otacon and his sister, Emma, have traumatic memories related to a swimming pool, and, well… I won’t spoil the game for you. Play it yourself!

Kyrie was an easy character to write (way easier than Taro, whose internal monologues make up the bulk of the game and had to be edited significantly for brevity/clarity/so that they didn’t sound like the kinds of vent tweets you see from your mutual who isn’t doing that well). They’re pretty effortlessly sound-minded and, although they have a great appreciation for life and culture of all shapes and sizes, basically a scholar through and through. I think they and Taro serve as two halves of a logic-emotion core to the game and I tried to have them contrast each other in basically every scene they’re in. Rather than Kyrie being purely “logical” and Taro being purely “emotional”, I wanted to show that people in real life are almost never as easily boiled down; in scenes where Taro is freaking out/having a breakdown/otherwise not doing well mentally, Kyrie is usually stern, the voice of reason, the catalyst for him to keep going “because you have to”. The inverse of this is that in scenes where Taro is being very “boring” and “no-nonsense”, Kyrie is there to lighten the mood. They’re sort of a chameleon in that way and once I got comfortable writing in their voice, I was excited to have so many scenes where Kyrie reads almost as Taro’s one tether to reality/the outside world. If Taro is insular, close-minded and egocentric, Kyrie is pretty social, open-minded and thinking about “the masses”. They’re capable of seeing the forest in spite of the trees when Taro isn’t able to because they lack the same level of neuroticism he has and are more willing to go with the flow.

I initially had a lot of concerns about Kyrie’s portrayal (which I conveyed to my editor and was reassured about); I tried to be pretty perceptive and careful about not portraying Kyrie as a part of the “manic pixie dream girl” archetype or as childish/juvenile. I think they’re simultaneously a pretty whimsical person and a down-to-earth realist who simply “sees the cup as half-full”. Because Taro is not in the best mental state, I was worried about falling into this trap of having Kyrie portrayed unconditionally as “more of a concept than a real individual”. I did the best I could and was told I did a good job, but I guess we’ll see how that turns out. There were some characterization moments in my initial drafts that didn’t make it to the final script (namely an extension of the scene on Day 1 in the supermarket in which Taro freaks out about Kyrie’s clothing and worries they’ll be too conspicuous in public; in hindsight I think it both does a good job of 1) establishing Taro as someone so concerned about how he’s perceived that he unintentionally forces his own insecurities onto others 2) establishing Kyrie as someone who doesn’t really mind, because they prefer to “go with the flow”) that I found pretty interesting for both of them. In hindsight a lot of the moments I cut are moments that I still think work well for the story but were redundant/I didn’t have time/some other reason… I’m happy overall with how my game came out, even if a lot of it was done under pressure or a bit more hastily than I’d have liked, and I have a lot to say about it. This Kyrie section is brief because I don’t want to spoil the game for people who haven’t played it. My bad.

I struggled a lot with Taro’s narratorial voice and worried that it came off as dweebish or belonging to someone so perpetually “down on himself” that it became a slog to read. He originally had a few much longer monologues that went on for pages and pages and pages and eventually Mira (over at detective.zone, the editor who pulled a 48-hour bender in order to edit the script and whom I owe pretty everything to, honestly; the game would not be finished or in a good state without her) told me to cut them down for length, which fixed a lot of the insecurities I was feeling about tone and readability and all that. All in all, I want to say there were easily *thousands* of comments and suggestions made for Have Mercy On Me’s script, we changed a lot—even down to very simple or forgettable changes, like single words being changed to their synonyms or commas being added/moved—and the editing process was super meticulous. It took about a week.

The prologue was the section that took the longest to write, mostly because of the subject matter. I was very strict with myself about the tone of it and didn’t want to make suicide out to be a laughing matter, but I’ve also just read and heard way too many sentiments about suicide that veer almost uncomfortably into something patronizing… I think that for a lot of people struggling with suicidal thoughts, the reason why many of them feel they need to resort to suicide is because they feel their lives are spinning out of their control. Suicide is, therefore, the suicidal person’s way of asserting control over their lives and trying to combat the feelings of helplessness. It was important to me to portray the suicide attempt at the beginning of the game in a way that I felt was pretty realistic (at least to my experiences), honest and vulnerable while also establishing who Taro is as a person, how he talks, how he feels on the inside, etc. I was nervous about this scene because the beginning of your story is always going to be someone’s first impression, right? I wondered a lot about what people would think of me or my game by opening with such an explicit conversation about suicide. I’m at a spot with it now where I think it reads about as well as it possibly could and is as honest as I could make it while also making it a somewhat pleasant reading experience.

The actual plotting process for HMOM was very haphazard, self-indulgent and fly-by-the-seat-of-my-pants. If I had an idea, I’d write it down as a bullet point in my notes app and come back to it later. Like I mentioned earlier, I did most of my brainstorming on walks and while listening to LOUD(!) music. Music was really the thing that brought this project together, in the end, both in the Watsonian and Doylist senses… I listened to a lot of what I listen to *now* as an adult, but also a ton of what I listened to as a kid, so my rotation for this was mostly Folie à Deux (2008), The Downward Spiral (1994), Hybrid Theory (2000), From Under the Cork Tree (2005), The Fragile (1999), Pinkerton (1996), The Bends (1995), Discipline (1981), The Wall (1979), The Black Parade (2006), some miscellaneous Nine Inch Nails, Korn, Joy Division, New Order, Silver Jews/Purple Mountains, yadda yadda yadda… I was interested in trying to revive my inner emo kid and also unite that inner emo kid with my present-day sad, brooding self. I bet Taro listened to so much Linkin Park…

If you can believe it, Have Mercy On Me didn’t really have anything to do with music until maybe two or three weeks into brainstorming/pre-planning. I had these characters and a very basic premise, but couldn’t think of much of anything for them to do. I’m pretty sure some of my early concepts had to do with Kyrie and Taro coming into contact with a government agency, Kyrie making a pact with Taro (like Chainsaw Man, I guess? I haven’t seen it!) and the two of them sharing a body. I actually still like this concept and would be open to rehashing it in the future, like in a DLC or side story or something. It eventually just kind of fell into place at one point, but I can’t really explain how… at some point I realized it’d be interesting to write a doomsday story with the clock ticking down on everything the characters did (and even at one point had a physical clock in the corner of the screen like Three O’Clock High (1987), High Noon (1952) or Persona 3), which gave me a little bit more relief; writing under that kind of constraint took a lot of the fear out of having to write so many words. Later on (I don’t remember when), I became pretty interested in the idea of the two characters getting together to form a band, kind of like Linda Linda Linda (2005) or even girl-idol franchises like Love Live!, Bandori, K-On, etc. Personally, the whole dynamic here really reminded me of Kyon and Haruhi (does anybody remember them..? no…?). After that, the rest of the story really started to fall into place. I’m not 100% pleased with all of the text itself, but I am really proud of and happy with the actual story/plot of the game and probably wouldn’t change it for anything. I feel like I ended up making something very true to my heart and passions, which can be hard to do!

Overall, I think what was most important to me with the writing was EARNESTNESS. English-language visual novels have a pretty bad reputation for being snarky, irony-poisoned, contemptuous towards their predecessors, etc and I really wanted to break that stereotype here. I don’t think language of origin should determine whether or not a story contains something meaningful or heartfelt, so in a way there was a little bit of (self-imposed) pressure for me to make the game as sincere as I could.

THE CODING PROCESS

Preface: you probably don’t want to copy a lot of my code for this game. This was my first real *anything*, I taught myself Python *while* making it (my home language is Java) and I don’t have any prior experience with Ren’Py. Looking back, a lot of HMOM is built on some pretty inefficient code in the sense that if I had done things differently, I would have made it a lot easier for myself; this is mostly because I just wasn’t thinking ahead, I didn’t have anyone to assess my code and I was mostly doing everything on the fly without planning it out sufficiently. I’m impressed with what I was able to accomplish given how incompetently the insides are put together, but I do think it’s something I’ll want to either adjust in the future or just use as a learning experience now that I actually know what I’m doing.

When I first started putting the game together, I had Narcissu on the mind and used it as inspiration in a few places (namely the layout of the whole thing; I find it pretty chic and minimalist, it leaves a lot to the imagination and provides you with some visuals and sound without overloading your senses or necessitating a lot of drawn assets). I’ve always been more of a NVL guy over the more-popular ADV-style visual novels and I think it works for HMOM. It might have been cool to have drawn sprites for Kyrie and Taro, but in the end it just wasn’t what I ended up pursuing; it took me several days just to design Kyrie and by then I realized my own art wasn’t really something I was impressed with or that I found fitting for the game. Because of Narcissu, I had been interested in coding my game in ONScripter or PONScripter (because I’m a hipster, I guess…?) but quickly realized that this would be a terrible, awful idea once the only people who still use PONScripter (Umineko modders!) informed me that they hate the engine, it’s not good, and that I’d be fighting with it more than working on my game. So… Ren’Py it was.

Music gameplay didn’t really become a thing until around October 22, VERY late into the jam. It was kind of just something I thought of on a whim one day, not really anything I’d planned in advance. I’m kind of proud of it (despite the sequencer’s jankiness) because I find it charming and unique. I sought out to integrate gameplay into the VN in a similar manner to Doki Doki Literature Club (in terms of quantity and prominence; i feel like that game does a good job of balancing the ratio of reading to gameplay) and I feel like that’s something I accomplished. I’m someone who enjoys reading a lot, but at one point I realized the game was resembling a book and wanted to throw something in there to shake things up a little bit.

My early builds looked bad! My early graphics looked worse! It took a while for me to find a visual aesthetic I was happy with, but I think what I ended up with is something I will be happy with even in the future. “Timelessness” was somewhat important to me here in the sense that I wanted to make something that I’ll be able to look back on later and not feel embarrassed of… I think I hit that sweet spot, but we’ll see how I feel about it in five(!) or even ten(!!!) years, hahahahaha…… Here are some examples of old, old, old, yucky WIPs.

Haha… um, yeah, as you can see, it took many iterations for me to find something I was happy with, and a lot of these aren’t really up to par. We got there eventually, though! I think!

I don’t consider myself a (very good) illustrator and didn’t feel like my art fit what I was going for, so I just didn’t include any! CLOSING THOUGHTS

I’m actually not sure there’s a ton to say that I haven’t already said… this was a really informative project for me as I’ve never attempted any kind of original writing before, have never attempted to make a video game before and tend to be a little flaky with my own personal projects (I’m a perfectionist and have trouble finishing things because of it). Largely due to the multi-day proofreading back-and-forth, Have Mercy On Me is in a spot that I feel I can be really proud of and hopefully look back on as a positive experience years from now. I’m really grateful to everyone who encouraged me along the way as well as the folks who are running the Denpa VN Jam as without them, I probably wouldn’t have ever made this to begin with. It felt nice to get in touch with my creative side, explore some things I’d pushed deep down inside of me and let them out. I hope that whoever is reading this either gains some insight into my creative process or some interest in the game by reading it! Shamelessly self-promoting again: Have Mercy On Me is now available on itch.io for free (or an optional donation), so feel free to pick it up if you’re interested in any of what I had to say here. Thanks for reading all of this!
