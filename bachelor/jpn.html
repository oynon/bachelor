Bachelor's Japanese resources, last updated 8/26/26
I taught myself to read, write and speak Japanese in less than a year using the resources I'll talk about here; much of this is adapted from my semi(?)-viral(?) Tumblr post on the topic, much of which was inspired by my discovery of learnjapanese.moe, another truly great site. The webmaster has a lot of expertise and experience that I don't have, so I suggest you check out some of her resources as well. I also love kuri's immersion guide, a page similar to this one.

Resources in bold are my personal favorites. I provide several alternatives when available; just pick your favorite.

Kana (phonetic building blocks)
  1. DJT kana game
  2. Kuuube's kana game
  3. Ved's kana game
  4. Tofugu's hiragana mnemonics
  5. Stroke order chart
  6. Tadoku readers (for practicing reading kana and furigana)
Vocabulary
  1. Anki and its setup guide (flashcards, REQUIRED... well, highly recommended!)
  2. Kaishi 1.5k, an Anki vocab deck
  3. Manabi, another vocab deck (I recommend this after Kaishi.)
  4. Lapis, my favorite anki notetype
Grammar

  1. Yokubi
  2. Tae Kim's Grammar Guide
  3. Imabi
  4. JLPT Grammar Cheat Sheet
  5. Cure Dolly (bite-sized grammar videos) and a transcript of all her videos
Software
  1. Yomitan and its setup guide (or any other pop-up dictionary, pretty much REQUIRED)
  2. Preparing your PC for mining vocab
  3. GameSentenceMiner, a tool for immersing with visual novels and video games. Has Yomitan and OCR built-in. Saved my life. Note that I use this almost exclusively for VNs
  4. Manatan, a tool for immersing with anime, manga, light novels and EPUBs. Has built-in dictionary lookup. Extremely painless; I use this for manga and upload my own raws from local files
  5. Yatsu, an e-book reader, has really good features including device sync
  6. Hoshi, an iOS reader meant to emulate ttsu
Recommendations + Finding Content
  1. jiten.moe, a list of many books, shows, visual novels and video games with difficulties and personalized Anki decks (I love this site)
  2. VN Club, like jiten but just for VNs. Has great difficulty listings and cool tools
  3. anikototv, an anime site where you can turn off subtitles and/or add your own with asbplayer
  4. asbplayer, a browser extension that lets you mine vocab from anime subtitles
  5. jpdb, a similar site to jiten.moe
  6. Japanese Media Recommendations, a spreadsheet by kuri
  7. My (Bachelor's) VN recommendation list
  8. this big ass sheet of starter visual novels, video games and manga
  9. this VN chart (ordered by difficulty from easiest to hardest... note that some "average" games are actually very difficult and this is kind of an optimistic chart lol)
  10. nyaa.si / sukebei (use qbittorrent and tor) has nearly everything
  11. Hakuneko has manga in JPN
  12. Anna's Archive has books
  13. Ryuugames has a fuckton of visual novels. Make sure you're downloading the game in Japanese and not a translation. If you go here tread very carefully because it has deceptive ads that look like download links
  14. It may seem crazy but this video improved my listening a lot.
Miscellaneous
  1. NihongoTracker, a browser-based immersion tracker with gamification elements. I use this literally every day.
  2. Tangotype, for practicing typing in Japanese
  3. Fudoki, a guide to Japan's prefectures (in Japanese or English with a toggle button)
